## pflegePro

A workplace health app for safe patient handling in elderly care
Healthcare · 2023

Source: `01 - Healthcare App.pdf` in Google Drive, folder "UX Case Studies".

### The problem
pflegePro is a German mobile learning app for care workers in elderly care (Altenpflege), part of workplace health management. Care workers often suffer from lower back pain, shoulder strain and lifting injuries, and the existing safety training felt too theoretical, hard to remember and disconnected from a real shift. The challenge: how might we make safety training easy to understand and apply during daily care routines?

### Research
I ran interviews, contextual inquiry and think-aloud usability sessions with 5 care workers: 2 registered nurses, 2 nursing assistants and 1 nursing student, aged 22 to 58. They completed four tasks: find a lesson on moving a patient from bed to wheelchair, replay a movement animation, save a lesson for later, and check their training progress. Three themes came out: time pressure (short lessons, quick access), fear of hurting patients or moving incorrectly, and a strong preference for visual, step-by-step guidance. The usability test surfaced three pain points: progress was hidden, the save icon wasn't recognized, and some screens had too much text.

Participants: 5
Baseline: task completion 62%, task success 58%, time on task 2m 48s, SUS 61, SEQ 4.1
Note: the PDF's usability test section lists 85% / 70% / 34s / SUS 82.5 / SEQ 5.37, which don't match the conclusion. The owner chose the conclusion's figures.

### The insight
Care workers already knew the theory; what they lacked was guidance short enough to use between shifts and visual enough to copy.

### What was made and why

**Progress on the home screen.** The home screen opens with a "Your programme" card showing the current lesson and a progress bar, instead of progress living in a separate section. Participants couldn't find their progress history and expected to see it straight away on the home screen.

**A heart to save, explained.** Saving uses a heart instead of a bookmark, and the empty Favorites screen explains it: once you mark something with a heart, you'll find it here. Some participants didn't recognize the bookmark icon at all.

**Short, video-first lessons.** Programmes and exercises lead with video, each lesson shows its length (40 sec, 1 min), and longer explanations sit behind "More info", instead of text-heavy screens. Participants said they wouldn't watch long videos between shifts and that less text would suit their working day.

### Outcome
Task completion rose from 62% to 91% and task success from 58% to 88%, time on task dropped from 2m 48s to 1m 22s, and the SUS score went from 61 to 86, in the excellent range. SEQ improved from 4.1 to 6.2. Next I'd add learning paths by role and experience, offline access for use on shift, and, longer term, posture feedback using the phone's sensors.

Left out on purpose: the PDF's claims of 25% fewer movement mistakes, recall up from 57% to 82%, and +41% confidence. With 5 participants they read as overclaimed.
