## SpringIn

An AI-assisted design sprint on how nurses ask for time off

### The problem
SpringIn started with a guess: nurses in German hospitals lose their time off because of how duty rosters are made (late plans, changes after publication, calls on days off). Before designing, I ran a sprint to find out whether scheduling was really where the problem started, and for whom.

### Research
Interviews with registered nurses (including a float pool nurse and a rarely-called-in negative case) and ward managers who build rosters, a written follow-up round, and desk research on German rostering rules and national staffing data. 5 participants, 8 hypotheses with written support/contradict criteria. The founding guess mostly didn't hold: most wishes were granted, and participants traced roster trouble to staffing more than to plan-making.

How I worked with AI: Claude as research and design partner; custom skills drafted the research frame, affinity map, insight statements, problem frames and Garrett layers; every AI synthesis flagged for my review; each decision logged with its reason; prototype built in Claude Design.

### The insight
A high approval rate hides the wishes nobody writes down. (P2: "I don't write all of them, because there are also other colleagues with family, and our head nurse has to consider them also.")

### What was made and why
**Reframing twice.** Frame 1 (paper swaps reach the system at month end) reached a wireframe that felt underwhelming, so back to research. Frame 2 (ward manager finding cover) paused: no more control for nurses. Frame 3 (a nurse leaves wishes unwritten for colleagues' sake) chosen: both claims trace to a participant's words, nurse at the center, reachable without more staff.

**A wish with room to move.** Up to 2 alternatives per wish, or "no alternative", instead of more wishes or fairness rules (co-determined with the works council, out of scope). Alternatives give the head nurse options instead of another constraint.

**Honest about what a wish is.** "A wish is not a promise"; no approval status before the plan is out; colleagues can't see each other's wishes (personal data); dead ends point to the head nurse or a colleague.

### Outcome
Delivered a clickable prototype, a design rationale tracing each decision to its source, and an implementation document. Not tested yet; the frame rests on one nurse's words. Next: test with more ward nurses, and with a head nurse to see whether alternatives make wishes easier to fit.
